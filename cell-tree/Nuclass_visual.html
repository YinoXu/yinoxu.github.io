01 / WITHIN-PATIENT TISSUE CONTEXT

Local context tracks the interface of each TLS.

Across 146 patients with at least eight measurable candidates, the median within-patient correlation between interface balance and surrounding epithelial/stromal balance is ρ = 0.402 (patient-bootstrap 95% CI 0.367–0.443). The direction is positive in 141 of 146 patients.

The interface measure compares the fraction of T–epithelial versus T–stromal neighbor connections that cross a candidate boundary, at a 25 µm distance cap. Surrounding context is the log-density balance of epithelial and stromal predictions in the outer 50 µm. This supports a local microenvironment explanation for within-patient variation. Both measurements share classifier-derived identities, so their agreement is descriptive evidence rather than independent biological confirmation.

Clinical lead: greater between-TLS interface heterogeneity is associated with higher overall-survival hazard in TRAIN: HR 1.43 (1.09–1.88), 138 patients / 56 events. With additional geometry, abundance and entropy adjustment, HR is 1.48 (1.15–1.92).

What the image tests: whether the same patient's lymphoid aggregates contact different stromal or epithelial neighborhoods. Epithelial predictions do not establish malignancy; stromal predictions do not identify CAFs. Candidate 28: raw detail · Candidate 1: raw detail

The two new masks include remeasured area, cell membership and composition; their areas are approximately 39–42% smaller than the original regions. A smoother boundary is not evidence of pathological accuracy. The cohort associations are not yet validated against this segmentation change. Other refinement attempts that spread into diffuse infiltration are retained as diagnostics and are not shown as successful segmentations. 02 / ENDOTHELIAL CONCENTRATION

A few TLS carry disproportionate endothelial density.

Among 161 patients with at least five measurable candidates, the highest-density approximately 20% of TLS account for a median 39.9% of summed core endothelial density.

For each patient, candidates are ranked by core endothelial density; the top ⌈0.2 × TLS count⌉ densities are summed and divided by the total. This measures how unevenly density is distributed across TLS. It is not the fraction of all endothelial cells in a patient. Soft-probability and hard-label concentration scores correlate at ρ = 0.924.

Clinical lead: greater core endothelial concentration is associated with higher disease-free-interval hazard: HR 2.56 (1.48–4.43), 85 TRAIN patients / 19 events. The additional-adjustment estimate is 2.68 (1.40–5.13).

Biological question: do these regions contain a distinctive vascular niche, or ordinary vessels sampled unevenly across aggregates? Endothelial predictions do not establish high endothelial venules. The 19-event sensitivity model has limited support and requires follow-up. Candidate 42: raw detail · Candidate 16: raw detail

03 / EXTERIOR PLASMA-CELL CONCENTRATION

Plasma-cell hotspots occupy a minority of TLS neighborhoods.

The highest-density approximately 20% of TLS account for a median 49.7% of summed exterior plasma-cell density in the same 161-patient measurable cohort.

Here the measurement uses the outer 50 µm neighborhood rather than the TLS core. Soft-probability and hard-label concentration scores correlate at ρ = 0.950. The same concentration definition can distinguish a patient with broadly distributed plasma cells from one with a small number of enriched neighborhoods.

Clinical lead: greater exterior plasma concentration is associated with lower disease-specific-survival hazard: HR 0.45 (0.23–0.89), 115 TRAIN patients / 23 events. The additional-adjustment estimate is 0.35 (0.12–0.99).

Biological question: are these focal neighborhoods linked to local antibody-producing activity? This is a spatial hypothesis; model-predicted plasma identity alone does not establish functional activity. Candidate 58: raw detail · Candidate 25: raw detail

All three hazards are per one training-feature SD after 1st/99th-percentile clipping, from cancer-stratified Cox models. Baseline adjustment includes age, sex, stage and conventional TLS measures. Additional adjustment includes TLS count, median area, classifier entropy and related mean abundance. These are post-selection nominal intervals from previously explored development patients, not independent validation or calibrated individual risk estimates. OS, DFI and DSS are distinct endpoints; their HRs are not directly comparable. All six fits.

04 / WHAT SUPPORTS A TLS TYPE?

Six descriptive patterns; biological types remain unestablished.

Composition differs between the six previously proposed patterns, but current evidence does not establish six reproducible biological TLS types.

Statistical structure

BIC favors six components in the tested 3–10 range. Separation is weak: silhouette = 0.108. A preferred mixture size does not demonstrate a natural type count.

Patient-resampling stability

Median comparison-fold ARI = 0.358, range 0.212–0.490. Per-pattern median Jaccard overlap ranges from 0.204 to 0.494. Membership is sensitive to the sampled patients.

Molecular support

No association among six pattern fractions × nine bulk RNA panels passes the 54-test FDR threshold of 0.05. The RNA profiles are patient-matched, not spatially matched to individual TLS.

We therefore prioritize continuous interface and concentration measurements. P1–P6 remain descriptive identifiers; labels such as “mature,” “GC,” “centroblast” or “centrocyte” are not justified by this clustering. Pattern-level evidence · All 54 RNA associations · All resampling results.

05 / COMPLETED MATURITY & MOLECULAR FOLLOW-UP

B-cell size organization is not yet a maturity marker.

In 53 within-patient, area-matched S versus E/P pairs, a new high-confidence B-cell analysis used 50 µm neighbor graphs and 99 shuffles within radial strata. Excess nuclear-size organization did not separate the predicted stages (paired p = 0.877; five-test q = 0.920). The earlier positive association was also not stable after geometry, B density and entropy adjustment. This does not support identifying centroblast/centrocyte states from size alone.

We additionally matched and verified 194 primary-tumor RNA profiles. None of the 117 prespecified imaging-feature × marker-panel associations passed FDR 0.05. The molecular follow-up is complete for these available profiles; it provides no corrected molecular confirmation of the nominated leads. Patient-matched bulk RNA cannot locate a signal within a specific TLS.

Measurement and statistical definitions

Cell type probabilities are retained rather than treating all labels as certain truth. The core is the inner half of maximum candidate depth; exterior 50 µm measurements use disjoint nearest-TLS tissue neighborhoods. Graph measurements use kNN12 with physical distance caps. The local 0.5 µm refiner uses image-dependent pixel evidence and spatial regularization; it is an exploratory algorithm, not a newly trained TLS neural network or pathology gold standard.

Patient-level hazard fits use TRAIN only. Within-patient correlations contribute one correlation per patient; confidence intervals resample patients 1,000 times. RNA partial-rank analyses adjust for histology, age, sex, advanced stage, TLS count and TLS area fraction. The imaging-panel family has 117 comparisons; the subsequent pattern-panel audit has a separately reported 54-comparison family. No adjusted p-value removes the effects of earlier adaptive hypothesis selection.

Examples were selected after reviewing predicted measurements for visible contrast and tissue quality, without selecting on survival. They illustrate the hypotheses and do not estimate overall segmentation accuracy. All shown cell/nuclear contours are actual masks. Local refinements and cohort statistics have explicit separate versions; no clinical association is presented as validated under the new local masks.
